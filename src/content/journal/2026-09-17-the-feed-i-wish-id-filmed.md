With the grandparents gone, these three weeks are the ones where the two of us found our footing — and where Jenn, week by week, came all the way back.

She changed her first *poop* diaper (a genuine rite of passage — the pee ones are practice; this is the real exam). She started taking the night feeds. And somewhere in there she mentioned she was feeling much better, which I felt too, in a quieter way: one evening it just landed that we'd stopped keeping score and started trying to make things a little easier for each other. After everything, that felt like the biggest milestone of all.

## He's a little person now

Cody has become genuinely *interactive*. He grabs the string of my hoodie and laughs — an actual laugh, aimed at me. There's a whole small human assembling himself in there.

He's also, apparently, a survivor. We tried to give him a bottle that might have turned — we'd lost track of the pump date — and he took one taste and flatly refused it. Good instincts, kid. We'll call that self-preservation and try not to think too hard about it.

## The birthday tour

We're lucky with how much life we still get to have. For my birthday we brought Cody along to a late lunch at Ryoko, and he sat in his car seat through the whole thing barely fussing — a proper restaurant baby. That evening we made the free happy-lamb hotpot deal; Eric came through and brought Donovan, so we were four. And the day before, Ryan and Kim turned out to be in town and treated us to Carino and Tsujiri — the mussels sauce as good as ever, plus the crispy-pork ramen, that gyoza-pasta fusion thing, and a tonkatsu carbonara. Generous friends, a very full table. Not bad for the birthday of a man who mostly measures time in feeds now.

## The perfect feed

And then, the one I keep thinking about. Jenn took him at 11 p.m., and he slept straight through until 4:50 a.m. — so soundly I had to wake him for his bottle. But he woke up *starving*: grabbing at the bottle with both hands, trying to hold it himself, and then he chugged a full 100 ml without spilling a single drop and without a single burp-up coming back. Guzzled the whole thing at once, and when it was gone he fell right back asleep — and smiled.

The easiest feed he's ever given us. After weeks of the spit-up wars, it felt like he was doing it on purpose, just to show us he could. I wish I'd had it on video. I wonder if he'll ever feed that well again. But it happened, once, perfectly — and now it's written down, which is almost as good.
