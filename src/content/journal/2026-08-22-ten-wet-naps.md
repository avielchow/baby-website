The big development this week is that Cody is *here* now, in a way he wasn't before. His awake windows have stretched out, and he spends them taking the world in — I've started walking slow circles around the bassinet just to watch his eyes track me the whole way around, like a little lighthouse beam following the boat.

There are cute vowels now, too, surfacing once in a while, and a real smile here and there. And — the photographer in me can't not notice this — his eyes have started to *twinkle*, a proper catchlight when the light hits them right. I wonder if it's because they're still so completely black; whatever it is, it undoes me every time.

## The one that took ten wet naps

He didn't poop for two full days. I'd made peace with it — apparently they slow down around now, which nobody warned me about — and then he let one go that will live in family legend. It took **ten wet naps** to clean up. Ten. But — and I want this on the permanent record — it did not escape the diaper. Not a drop. We contained the situation. Small victories are still victories.

## Godmother, and the flying lessons

He got his first big out-of-town visitor: his godmother, Jenn's best friend and old roommate, in from Vancouver. She took one look at him, scooped him into a full Superman pose — one arm forward, cape optional — and "flew" him around the house. He did not object. First flight, logged.

## The squat mystery

Here is a genuine scientific finding from Week 5: doing squats while holding him stops the crying. Not swaying, not bouncing — *squats*. I have no idea why. I only know that at some point I became a man doing weighted air squats in a dark living room at 2 a.m. because the weight in my arms demands it, and it works, and I've stopped asking questions.

We also cracked the code on feeding ourselves when the grandparents step out: frozen wontons. An honest meal, ten minutes, no thought required. Week five is the week we started to feel like we might actually be running this place.
