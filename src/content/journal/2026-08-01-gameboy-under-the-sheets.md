In one frame the glasses are perched right on his sleeping face and he looks deeply unimpressed by the whole production. One day he'll probably assume these were AI — but we loved making them for real, by hand, with an actual scarf and a real, very sleepy wizard.

He's more *here* this week. He stays awake longer and stares at everything like he's marvelling at a world that just switched on. I filed his nails for the first time and could barely pry his fists open — the grip on this kid, especially mid-cry, is something else.

## The night shift is mine

Here's the honest part. The nights are hard, and right now they're all mine. I take 12 to 8, alone, and somewhere around 3 a.m. — when he's been crying for a while and only settles if he's held — I hit a wall of frustration I didn't expect to feel. What surprises me most is that it's *only* at night. In daylight none of it lands the same way. I've been turning it over: some quiet, tired part of me even wonders if a bit of that late-night edge is just evolution's way of keeping a dad awake when he's needed. I don't have the answer. But naming it helps, and by morning it's always gone.

## A house that believes babies shouldn't cry

The grandparents have been a gift — genuinely. Jenn's parents have taken Cody so completely that I now sleep 8 to 11, morning and night, and just hold down the overnight. At first I'll admit it stung; there were so many instructions that it started to feel like every move I made was wrong, like I was being gently edged out of my own kid. Then I stopped resisting and just… let them love him. Best decision of the week.

It comes with house rules, though. There's a firm belief in this home — I've seen it in Japanese parenting videos too — that a baby should not cry in the first month, and that if he does, someone has done something wrong. So every time Cody cries I feel less like a dad with a fussy newborn and more like a teenager hiding a B+ on his report card, bracing for an unimpressed grandma. We also have to *hide the milk bottles*. I am a grown man sneaking formula around my own house like a kid playing Gameboy under the sheets after lights-out. I regret nothing.

## The week's plot twists

We ended up in Labour &amp; Delivery again — this time for Jenn, some blood clots that turned out to be nothing serious. As always the L&D team took beautiful care of her; the only villain was the six-hour wait. The upside was a genuinely lovely night: the grandparents had Cody (and Jenn had pumped enough that they gave him his first two bottle feeds, 100 ml each), so we snuck in a Happy Hour meal at Hello Nori — our **first meal out** since he arrived. Jenn even scored a free baby food processor off the Buy Nothing group on the drive home. We floated in the door feeling like we'd gotten away with something… and discovered the AC had died. Of course it had.

Then we splurged — a Snoo and a Switch 2, and Jenn's already deep into a new Pokémon game. Cody's getting fussier at night and wants to be held through it, so we're quietly praying the Snoo earns its keep.

## Small things, happy things

- Watermelon, $8.99 at Safeway, picked up by the in-laws, who then cooked my favourite ribs. That's the whole joy, right there.
- Coco has reclaimed the master bedroom as her personal kingdom and, a little lonely, now sprints into the bed to cuddle the second I lie down. There are groggy moments where I wake up unsure whether I fell asleep holding Cody or whether it's the cat — so far, always the cat.
- Dad-brain highlight: I tried to microwave blueberries. I have no memory of why.

One week older, a little more awake, and surrounded — loudly, lovingly, bottle-smugglingly — by people who adore him. We'll take it.
