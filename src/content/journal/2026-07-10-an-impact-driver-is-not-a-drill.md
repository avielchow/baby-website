The final weeks before Baby have been busy but quite magical. I was very lucky to take two weeks off at the same time as Jenn, and to step back from the Masters program and just focus on the home. (This is Dad typing, for the record — the one who, until this month, had never used a drill other than the Ikea one.)

It began with the blackout curtains for the master bedroom. I bought what I believed was a drill, put in an honest effort at installing the curtains, and could not understand why nothing was working — until I discovered I had accidentally bought an impact driver instead. A learning adventure, is what I'm calling it.

<!-- photos: home projects / curtains / gallery wall -->

After the curtains we made a gallery wall in the upstairs hallway, and we're so happy with how it came out. We used the STAS hanging system, which lets us swap and rearrange any mix of frame and picture styles. For two people with permanent decision paralysis about design, a system where nothing is a final decision is a small miracle.

Then the yard. We bought a new $25 columnar aspen to plant where a dead tree stood — and after a full day of digging at the old stump, I learned that a stump is basically an iceberg. The stump, as of this writing, is winning.

Some days before Baby's arrival the nursery finally came together — a fake olive tree and picture frames from Ikea really pulled the room together. Many, many Ikea trips later, we feel great about it. We'd also been watching design videos and learned that lighting a room from different directions and heights, highlighting areas of interest, is what pulls a space together — so we started buying up lamps, which was honestly fun. Two minutes before Jenn's induction we were still running around HomeSense buying furniture, and found two jade stone lamps for the master bedroom that we love.

<!-- photos: nursery / lamps -->

And then, the most optimistic project of them all: a photo studio for Baby. We got a free, ultra-large bean bag to make a surface a baby can sink into, went to Fabricland for the first time ever for stretchy fabric to pull over it, set up my old Neewer Z2 flashes from a friend's clinic photoshoot, and installed a new background-paper system. We now have a little studio, and we are excited — though it may be hard to find the time, and I'm not confident how well it will work out.

<!-- photos: studio build -->

I also found time for three photoshoots, after so long without any. The engagement shoot was the challenging one — tough light, and the site was flooded so my usual spots were gone; improvising angles in backup locations is something I'll have to think more about. The other two were in my usual spot and were pure fun. My favourite was a family of five: twin photos, family photos, individual photos — multiple photoshoots rolled into one, and the whole family had a great time.

In between all of it, we've had the energy to actually go out and eat. The highlight: a pan-Asian place called Wok & Co — a passion project, it turns out, from the same owners as Namo. Jenn chatted with the owners, the curry was great, and we had a lovely time.

Any day now, all of this — the lamps, the wall, the little studio — gets its reason.
