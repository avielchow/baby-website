The Snoo is magic. I'd put Cody down crying in the Halo, moved him over to the Snoo out of desperation, and he slept for two straight hours. He came out of it *starving* — we had to drag him from his $2,000 bed kicking and screaming, and the second the bottle hit, he was chugging like a bear out of hibernation.

The bed did not stay his for long. Within the day, Coco had staged a coup and installed herself inside it, just her face peeking over the mesh wall like a landlord inspecting a unit she has decided is now hers.

## My turn to cook

For two weeks straight the grandparents have cooked for us, every single day. So this week I finally got to hand it back: a whole meal off the grill — peri-peri chicken legs, corn, and some prime rib steaks I found for ten dollars that came off so juicy I couldn't believe our luck. The parents loved it. After everything they've done, getting to put a good plate in front of *them* for once felt like the best thing I did all week.

## Feeding is still a fight

We've been struggling with the bottle since week one, and right now the hard part is the spit-up — endless spit-up. Twice this week it escalated into a full projectile vomit, enormous ones; the Monday-night edition arrived just as I pulled the diaper off (he'd already peed and pooped for good measure) and redecorated both Cody and my blanket. The public-service announcement I did not expect to be making: the stinkiest thing about a newborn is not the poop — it's the puke. We've started adjusting how we feed him to get ahead of it. He's a small boy with a big arm.

## The rhythm we've found

The thing that's really settled this week is life with the grandparents. We've found our groove, and honestly I'm amazed at them — they're positive and genuinely excited about the whole thing, right down to the diaper changes. At the end of the day I'm just grateful: I get to sleep, I don't have to think about food, and Cody is adored around the clock. We're lucky to have them here for this first month.

We also had our first visitor — Nhu came by, and it was such a small, ordinary, wonderful thing. For an hour I got to think about non-baby problems again, and I'd forgotten how good that felt. (I'm also, already, going to miss the swaddles when the time comes.)

And we took Cody on his **first walk in the carrier**. He slept through the entire outing — through the $2 Koi slushes (one day I'll tell him "back in our day, this slush was two bucks," and it'll cost him ten), through an impromptu stop at Jelaine's for a lovely visit, through all of it. Dead to the world, strapped to my chest, along for the ride.

## Tummy time

We started tummy time this week, and the surprise is how hard he already *tries* to lift his head. He gets it up — and then it rebounds, flopping straight back down to the mat or into your shoulder, which is somehow both hilarious and a little bit alarming to watch. His first real workout, and it's all on film.
