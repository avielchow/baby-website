Jenn took her first full night shift this week — and changed her first diaper — and I slept **six consecutive hours**. I'd forgotten what that felt like. I woke up genuinely disoriented by how good it was. It's a small line in the ledger, but it's a big one too: after a delivery that took so much out of her, this was Jenn stepping in on her own terms, at her own pace, and it meant the nights aren't only mine anymore.

## One whole minute

We did tummy time on my chest, and Cody lifted his head and *held* it — almost a full minute, no crying, no fussing, just a little wobbly weightlifter deciding he wanted to see the room. A few weeks ago his head lifted and rebounded straight back down like it was on a spring. This week he held the rep. You can watch him getting stronger in real time, and it's one of the best shows going.

## Two lobsters and a long goodbye

And then the week we knew was coming: Jenn's parents went home.

They've been here since the very beginning — since the 36-hour delivery, since the week I was running on one broken hour of sleep — and they carried us. So we sent them off the only right way: a big lobster dinner. Two 2.2-pounders from T&T, sixty dollars that day, which felt like a steal for a farewell feast. They ended up staying the night, and we played 80 points around the table like old times, the way we did before any of this.

Jenn is so sad to see them go — they've been her parents *and* her lifeline these weeks — and honestly so am I. But sitting at that table, full of lobster, watching everyone laugh over a card game with Cody asleep nearby, all I could think was how far we've come from that first week. We were barely standing then. Now we're hosting.
